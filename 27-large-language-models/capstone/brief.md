# 27 · capstone: your own assistant pipeline

## goal

take a language model from raw text to an aligned assistant, and document what you made. you pretrain a small `Llama` (topic 02) on a filtered, deduplicated corpus (topic 01), teach it to follow instructions with supervised fine-tuning (topic 03), align it to a preference with DPO (topic 04), sample its answers (topic 05), evaluate every stage with intervals and paired comparisons over several seeds (topic 06), and write a model card. the result is tiny and often wrong, and that's part of the point: at this scale you can watch each stage do its job, see what it can't do, and practise the honesty about both that a model card demands.

## data

- **pretraining:** tiny Shakespeare from `mlfs.datasets.tiny_shakespeare()` (cached under `~/.cache/mlfs/`). the first 90% is the pretraining text, split into speeches at blank lines, filtered with your `quality_filter` (at least 5 words) and deduplicated with your `minhash_dedup`, joined with `<|endoftext|>`. the last 10% is held out.
- **tokenizer:** your `BPETokenizer` with 257 entries: the 256 bytes plus the special token `<|endoftext|>` (no merges, so every character is a token and the skills below are learnable letter by letter).
- **instructions (synthetic):** generated by `make_tasks` from templates: *Continue: (first half of a line)* → the line's second half; *Write (word) in capitals.* → the word in capitals; *How many letters are in (word)?* → the count. training and test lines and words are disjoint (a stable hash puts 20% of lines and words in the test split; test lines also come only from the held-out tenth).
- **preferences (synthetic):** there are no human preferences on this machine. `gold_reward` plays the human: 1 or 0 for exact skill answers; for continuations, (fraction of words in the play's vocabulary) × (fraction of distinct words) − 0.15 × |number of words − the true ending's|. pairs of the SFT model's own samples are labelled by it.
- offline (`MLFS_OFFLINE=1`) the notebook runs the small configuration on a synthetic stand-in; the tests use tiny Llamas on text they generate.

## deliverables

1. `capstone.py` with the functions below implemented and their checks green:
   - `prepare_corpus(text, tokenizer, *, min_words=5, filter=True, dedup=True, seed=0)`: blank-line-separated documents → `quality_filter(doc, min_words=min_words)` (skipped if `filter=False`) → `minhash_dedup(..., seed=seed)` (skipped if `dedup=False`) → the kept documents in order, each followed by `<|endoftext|>`, encoded; returns `(ids int64 ndarray, stats)` with `stats` = `{n_docs, n_filtered, n_duplicates, n_kept, n_tokens}`.
   - `make_tasks(text, n, *, split='train', seed=0)`: `n` task dicts `{kind, prompt, answer}` as above, kinds drawn uniformly, deterministic for a seed; `ValueError` for another split. continue tasks use lines of 8–14 words (not speaker names) split at the middle word; skill tasks use lowercase words of 3–7 letters; train and test splits share no line and no word.
   - `gold_reward(task, response, vocab)`: the rule above (skills compare `response.strip()` with the answer; words are runs of `[A-Za-z']`, matched lowercased against `vocab`; an answer with no words scores −1).
   - `respond(model, tokenizer, prompt, *, n=1, max_new_tokens=32, temperature=1.0, generator=None, device='cpu')`: `n` samples continuing `"User: {prompt}\nAssistant:"` (your `generate_cached`, at most `block_size` tokens in total), each cut after the first `<|endoftext|>`: a list of `(token ids, decoded text without the end token, stripped)`.
   - `sft(model, tokenizer, tasks, *, steps, lr=1e-3, batch_size=32, seed=0, device='cpu')`: AdamW (no weight decay) on batches of `build_sft_example` / `collate_sft` / `sft_loss`, tasks drawn with `np.random.default_rng(seed)`, gradient clipping at 1; returns the per-step losses (floats) and leaves the model in eval mode.
   - `preference_pairs(model, tokenizer, tasks, vocab, *, k=8, seed=0, device='cpu')`: `k` samples per task (one CPU `torch.Generator` seeded with `seed`), paired as (0, 1), (2, 3), ...; the sample with the higher gold reward is `chosen`, ties are dropped; returns dicts `{prompt, chosen, rejected}` (token lists).
   - `dpo_train(policy, reference, tokenizer, pairs, *, steps, beta=0.1, lr=5e-5, batch_size=16, seed=0, device='cpu')`: freezes the reference's parameters, then `steps` calls of your `dpo_step` on random batches of pairs (AdamW, no weight decay); returns the list of `dpo_step` metric dicts and leaves the policy in eval mode.
   - `evaluate_assistant(model, tokenizer, tasks, vocab, *, reference=None, held_out=None, seed=0, device='cpu', eval_batches=20)`: a dict of `(value, lo, hi)`: `skill_acc` (greedy exact match on the skill tasks, `accuracy_ci`), `gold` (one sample per continue task, generator seeded with `seed`, mean with a 95% bootstrap interval), `win_rate` against `reference` if given (the reference answers with an identically seeded generator; ties count 1/2), and `lm_loss` on `held_out` if given (`estimate_loss`, interval `nan`).
   - `model_card(name, config, results, notes='')`: markdown with the sections *model details, intended use, training data, training procedure* (the `config` as a readable list, not a raw dump), *evaluation* (a table of every result as `value [lo, hi]`, or the value alone when it has no interval), *limitations* (every stage-to-stage drop in `skill_acc` and rise in `lm_loss`, with the numbers, and what the evaluation doesn't cover) and *ethical considerations*, naming the synthetic data as such and including `notes`.
2. the full-config run, and the write-up at the end of the notebook answering its five prompts, with your edited model card.

## what the reference gets

measured once on an Apple M2 (`mps`), full config. the corpus: 6,283 speeches, 486 removed by the quality filter (all too short: a speaker name and a few words, such as "All: Speak, speak."; 23 of them also fail the word-length rule), 3 near-duplicates, 5,794 kept: 987,292 byte tokens. none of the 97 test lines shares an 8-gram with the pretraining text. the preference step produced 2,791–2,824 pairs per seed from 800 prompts × 8 samples (ties dropped).

**pretraining.** 2,411,136 parameters, about 8 min, held-out loss **1.78–1.79** nats per byte (train 1.10). an earlier run with 3,000 steps overfit badly: held-out loss was lowest (1.82) around step 1,200 and climbed to 2.53 by step 3,000 while the training loss fell to 0.6, which is why the full config stops at 1,200 (the model has no dropout, and 3,000 steps are 25 passes over the corpus).

**post-training**, evaluated on 300 held-out tasks (about 100 of each kind). GPU runs are not bit-reproducible: rerunning the same notebook with the same seeds on `mps` moves every number, sometimes beyond its item interval, so the numbers below come from three complete runs of the full config (two by the author, one by a reviewer), each with seeds 0, 1, 2. the table shows the latest run, 95% bootstrap intervals over items for seed 0 and the range over its three seeds:

| stage | skill accuracy (greedy, exact) | gold reward on continuations | win rate vs previous stage | held-out loss |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| base | 0.00 (all seeds) | 0.605 [0.567, 0.643]; seeds 0.61–0.63 | – | 1.793 |
| SFT | 0.793 [0.734, 0.847]; seeds 0.79–0.90 | 0.641 [0.600, 0.681]; seeds 0.61–0.64 | 0.557 [0.459, 0.649]; seeds 0.50–0.58 | 2.69–2.76 |
| DPO | 0.714 [0.655, 0.773]; seeds 0.71–0.82 | **0.791** [0.759, 0.824]; seeds 0.66–0.79 | **0.722** [0.639, 0.799]; seeds 0.60–0.72 | 3.13–3.25 |

across all nine (run, seed) pairs:

- **SFT teaches the format and the skills**: the base model answers no skill question exactly (it continues the text instead), the SFT model 79–95% of them, on words it never saw in training. its continuations improve on the gold rule only a little, and its win rate against the base model straddles 0.5: finishing a line was already something the base model could do.
- **DPO moves the preference it was trained on**: its win rate against the SFT model was 0.57–0.72 in the author's two runs, and the 95% interval excluded 0.5 in 6 of the 9 (run, seed) pairs (3 of 3 in the latest run; 2 of 3 and 1 of 3, plus a borderline one, in the others). gold reward rose by 0.01–0.16 over SFT across all nine (run, seed) pairs. the effect is real but its size varies with the seed and the run at least as much as the item intervals suggest: report both.
- **alignment has costs that the gold rule doesn't see.** DPO lowered skill accuracy in every one of the nine (run, seed) pairs, by 3.4–13.3 points. in the latest run the paired differences (DPO − SFT, bootstrap over the same items) were −0.079 [−0.128, −0.030], −0.034 [−0.084, +0.015] and −0.084 [−0.128, −0.039]: two of three exclude zero. DPO's pairs are all continuations, so nothing anchors the skills. every post-training stage also raises the held-out language-modelling loss (1.79 → about 2.7 → about 3.2 nats per byte): the assistant is a worse model of Shakespeare than the base, the forgetting that topic 03's lab measured. mixing SFT and pretraining data into the DPO stage, a larger $\beta$ or fewer steps would trade some gold reward for less damage.
- the notebook prints a few answers from each model, marked as **illustrative**: single samples, which change from run to run. in every run, capitalizing a seen pattern mostly works, letter counts are often off by one, and continuations remain Shakespeare-flavoured nonsense. a 2.4M-parameter model trained on 1M characters can't write sense; the pipeline, not the prose, is the deliverable.

## stretch goals

- **LoRA instead of full SFT.** fine-tune with your `apply_lora` at rank 8 and 32; how close does it get, on the skills and on the continuations (topic 03's lab suggests where it won't)?
- **PPO against a learned reward model.** train a `RewardModel` on the same pairs and run topic 04's PPO loop; compare gold reward against KL with DPO's.
- **retrieval.** add a "Who speaks this line?" task answered from a `VectorIndex` over speeches (topic 07); measure how retrieval recall caps the answers.
- **speculative decoding for the assistant.** distil a 1-layer draft on the SFT model's outputs and measure acceptance and speed (topic 05).
- **contamination audit.** plant a copy of the test lines in the pretraining text, retrain, and check that your evaluation and `contamination_flags` catch it (topic 06).
